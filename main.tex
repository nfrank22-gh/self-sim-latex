\section{The self-similar framework}\label{sec:framework}


\subsection{Rescaling into a self-similar frame}\label{ssec:fw-rescaling}

Let $u_i^*(\mathbf{x}^*, t^*)$, $i = 1, \dots, d$, satisfy an evolution
equation of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-general-pde}
  \frac{\partial u_i^*}{\partial t^*} = R_i^*(u^*),
\end{equation}
on $\mathbb{R}^d$. We rescale amplitude, length, and time by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-nondim}
  u_i = \frac{u_i^*}{U}, \qquad
  x_j = \frac{x_j^*}{L}, \qquad
  t = \frac{t^*}{s(t)},
\end{equation}
where the amplitude scale $U$ and the length scale $L$ depend on the non dimensional time $t$. The timescale
is determined by specifiying the clock
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-clock}
  \tau = \frac{\mathrm{d}t^*}{\mathrm{d}t}.
\end{equation}
The clock $\tau$ is problem specific and can contain the time dependent length and amplitude scales.
It is defined in \S\ref{ssec:1d-clock} and \S\ref{ssec:ns-clock} for the two cases considered here.
\par
Throughout, a prime denotes differentiation with respect to $t$ and we use the following abbreviations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-rates}
  \sigma_L = \frac{L^\prime}{L}, \qquad \sigma_U = \frac{U^\prime}{U}.
\end{equation}
Differentiating $x_j = x_j^*/L$ with respect to $t$ at fixed $x_j^*$ gives,
for each component $j$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-dxdt}
  \frac{\mathrm{d} x_j}{\mathrm{d} t} = -x_j^* \frac{L^\prime}{L^2} = -x_j\, \sigma_L .
\end{equation}
Applying the chain rule to the left-hand side of \eqref{eq:fw-general-pde},
and using \eqref{eq:fw-clock} and \eqref{eq:fw-dxdt},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-lhs-chain}
  \begin{aligned}
  \frac{\partial u_i^*}{\partial t^*}
  &= \frac{\mathrm{d} t}{\mathrm{d} t^*}\left(
      \frac{\partial (U u_i)}{\partial t}
      + U \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} \frac{\mathrm{d} x_j}{\mathrm{d} t}
    \right) \\
  &= \frac{U}{\tau}\left(
      \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t}
      + \sigma_U u_i
      - \sigma_L\, x_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}
    \right).
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Equating \eqref{eq:fw-lhs-chain} to $R_i^*$ and solving for
$\partial u_i / \partial t$ gives the result used throughout this document.
Under the rescaling \eqref{eq:fw-nondim}, the equation \eqref{eq:fw-general-pde} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-lemma}
  \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t}
  = \underbrace{\sigma_L\, x_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}
    - \sigma_U\, u_i}_{\text{scaling terms}}
  + \underbrace{\frac{\tau}{U}\, R_i^*(U u,\, L x)}_{\text{physical terms}} .
\end{equation}
\par

Accordingly we split \eqref{eq:fw-lemma} into two flows. The \emph{scaling
operator} $\Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{S})}$ advances the scaling terms,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-scaling-flow}
  \Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{S})}(u_i)
  = u_i + \int_t^{t + \Delta t}
    \left( \sigma_L\, x_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} - \sigma_U\, u_i \right)
    \mathrm{d} t,
\end{equation}
and the \emph{physics operator} $\Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{P})}$ advances the
rest,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-physics-flow}
  \Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{P})}(u_i)
  = u_i + \int_t^{t + \Delta t} \frac{\tau}{U}\, R_i^*(U u,\, L x)\, \mathrm{d} t .
\end{equation}
Both are written as the field plus the integral of the terms they advance.
$\Phi^{(\mathrm{S})}$ is obtained in closed form below and is shared;
$\Phi^{(\mathrm{P})}$ is equation-specific and is supplied in
\S\ref{ssec:1d-physics-step} and \S\ref{ssec:ns-physics-step}.

\subsection{The scaling operator}\label{ssec:fw-scaling-operator}

Evaluating $\Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{S})}$ amounts to solving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-scaling-pde}
  \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t}
  = \sigma_L\, x_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} - \sigma_U\, u_i .
\end{equation}
This is a linear, first-order PDE and can be solved by the method of
characteristics. Note that the scaling terms do not couple the components of
$u_i$, so the argument below is componentwise.

Let $x_j(t)$ denote a characteristic curve satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-characteristic}
  \frac{\mathrm{d} x_j}{\mathrm{d} t} = -x_j\, \sigma_L.
\end{equation}
 The total derivative of $u_i$ along such a
curve is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-total-derivative}
  \frac{\mathrm{d} u_i}{\mathrm{d} t}
  = \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j} \frac{\mathrm{d} x_j}{\mathrm{d} t}
  = \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t} - \sigma_L\, x_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j},
\end{equation}
so along the characteristic \eqref{eq:fw-scaling-pde} reduces to the ordinary
differential equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-scaling-ode}
  \frac{\mathrm{d} u_i}{\mathrm{d} t} = -\sigma_U\, u_i .
\end{equation}

Integrating \eqref{eq:fw-characteristic} from $t_k$ to $t_{k+1}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-char-integrated}
  \ln x_j(t_{k+1}) - \ln x_j(t_k) = -\bigl[\ln L_{k+1} - \ln L_k\bigr]
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  x_j(t_{k+1}) = x_j(t_k)\, \frac{L_k}{L_{k+1}},
\end{equation}
so that the characteristic arriving at the evaluation point
$x_j \equiv x_j(t_{k+1})$ originated at
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-char-origin}
  x_j(t_k) = x_j\, \frac{L_{k+1}}{L_k} .
\end{equation}
Integrating \eqref{eq:fw-scaling-ode} gives
\begin{equation}
  u_i(t_{k+1}) = u_i(t_k)\, U_k / U_{k+1}.
\end{equation}
Combining this with the characteristic origin \eqref{eq:fw-char-origin} and the initial condition
$u_i(t_k, \cdot)$ gives the scaling operator in closed form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-scaling-closed}
  \Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{S})}(u_i)(\mathbf{x})
  = \frac{U_k}{U_{k+1}}\, u_i\!\left( \mathbf{x}\, \frac{L_{k+1}}{L_k} \right).
\end{equation}
Writing the step ratios
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-ratios}
  \tilde{U}_{k+1} = \frac{U_{k+1}}{U_k}, \qquad
  \tilde{L}_{k+1} = \frac{L_{k+1}}{L_k},
\end{equation}
this is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-scale-step}
  \Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{S})}(u_i)(\mathbf{x})
  = \frac{1}{\tilde{U}_{k+1}}\, u_i\!\left( \mathbf{x}\, \tilde{L}_{k+1} \right).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The scaling terms in \texorpdfstring{$\mathbf{k}$}{k}-space.}
For equations formulated spectrally it is useful to have the transform of the
scaling terms. Using $\widehat{\cdot}$ for the spatial Fourier transform,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-fourier-convention}
  \hat{u}_i(\mathbf{k}) = \int u_i(\mathbf{x})\, e^{-\mathrm{i} k_j x_j}\, \mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},
\end{equation}
and writing the spread term in conservative form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-scaling-divform}
  x_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}
  = \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left( x_j u_i \right) - d\, u_i,
\end{equation}
gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-fourier-scaling}
  \widehat{x_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}}
  = \mathrm{i} k_j \left( \mathrm{i} \frac{\partial \hat{u}_i}{\partial k_j} \right) - d\, \hat{u}_i
  = -\left( k_j \frac{\partial \hat{u}_i}{\partial k_j} + d\, \hat{u}_i \right),
\end{equation}
so that the transform of \eqref{eq:fw-scaling-pde} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-scaling-pde-fourier}
  \frac{\partial \hat{u}_i}{\partial t}
  = -\sigma_L \left( k_j \frac{\partial \hat{u}_i}{\partial k_j} + d\, \hat{u}_i \right)
  - \sigma_U\, \hat{u}_i .
\end{equation}
Rather than solving \eqref{eq:fw-scaling-pde-fourier} directly in
$\mathbf{k}$-space, it is easier to transform to physical space, apply
\eqref{eq:fw-scale-step}, and transform back; the Fourier transform
\eqref{eq:fw-fourier-convention} is linear, so the two are equivalent.

\subsection{Spectral interpolation}\label{ssec:fw-interpolation}

The closed form \eqref{eq:fw-scale-step} requires $u_i$ at the rescaled points
$\mathbf{x}\,\tilde{L}_{k+1}$, which are not grid points. On a uniform
periodic grid the represented field is band-limited, so it can be evaluated at
arbitrary points exactly, with no interpolation error, by summing its
truncated Fourier series there:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-spectral-interp}
  u_i\!\left(\mathbf{x}\, \tilde{L}_{k+1}\right)
  = \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{K}} \hat{u}_i(\mathbf{k})\,
    \exp\!\left( \mathrm{i}\, k_j x_j\, \tilde{L}_{k+1} \right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{K}$ is the set of represented wavenumbers and
$\hat{u}_i(\mathbf{k})$ are the normalized transform coefficients of $u_i$.
This is the defining property of the scheme: the scaling step introduces no
spatial discretization error of its own.

\paragraph{Queries outside the box.}
The true problem we are trying to approximate is posed on an infinite box, with $u_i$ going to zero as it approaches the boundaries.
For $\tilde{L}_{k+1} > 1$ it is possible that $|x_j\,\tilde{L}_{k+1}| > H/2$, in which case we would like the rescaling in \eqref{eq:fw-spectral-interp} to return $0$.
However, this is not the current behavior: instead, it wraps around the periodic box and returns a potentially nonzero value from the other end of the domain.
Therefore, to obtain the desired behavior we modify the scaling step to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-dilate-cutoff}
  u_i\!\left(\mathbf{x}\, \tilde{L}_{k+1}\right)
  = \chi\!\left(\mathbf{x}\, \tilde{L}_{k+1}\right)
    \sum_{\mathbf{k} \in \mathcal{K}} \hat{u}_i(\mathbf{k})\,
    \exp\!\left( \mathrm{i}\, k_j x_j\, \tilde{L}_{k+1} \right),
  \qquad
  \chi(\mathbf{y}) = \prod_{d} \mathbb{1}\!\left[\, |y_d| \le H/2 \,\right].
\end{equation}

\subsection{Constraint functionals}\label{ssec:fw-constraints}
We will set the ratios $\tilde{U}_{k+1}, \tilde{L}_{k+1}$ that the
scaling step \eqref{eq:fw-scale-step} depends on by requiring
the rescaled field to carry prescribed values of two functionals $F_1, F_2$ of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-homogeneity}
  F_m\!\left[ \frac{1}{\tilde{U}}\, u\!\left( x \tilde{L} \right) \right]
  = \tilde{U}^{-p_m}\, \tilde{L}^{-q_m}\, F_m[u],
  \qquad m = 1, 2 .
\end{equation}
The targets $F_1, F_2$ are fixed once, from the initial condition
(\S\ref{ssec:alg-init}). Given a field $u$ with current values
$F_m^* = F_m[u]$, imposing $\tilde{U}^{-p_m} \tilde{L}^{-q_m} F_m^* = F_m$ and
taking logarithms gives the following linear system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-constraint-system}
  \begin{bmatrix} p_1 & q_1 \\ p_2 & q_2 \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix} \ln \tilde{U} \\ \ln \tilde{L} \end{bmatrix}
  = \begin{bmatrix} \ln F_1^* - \ln F_1 \\ \ln F_2^* - \ln F_2 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
The functionals themselves are equation-specific
(\S\ref{ssec:1d-moments}, \S\ref{ssec:ns-constraints}); the substep that
applies \eqref{eq:fw-constraint-system} is \textsc{ConstrainedScale}
(\S\ref{ssec:alg-substeps}).

\subsection{Power-law ansatz and the step in physical time}\label{ssec:fw-powerlaw}

The algorithm also needs to \emph{predict} scale ratios over a fraction of a
step, at points where no constraint is available to fix them
(\S\ref{ssec:alg-strang}). For this we assume that both scales follow a power
law in physical time,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-powerlaw}
  X = (t^* + \alpha_X)^{\beta_X}, \qquad X \in \{U, L\},
\end{equation}
so that the ratio over a fraction $i$ of step $k$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-ratio}
  \frac{X_{k+i}}{X_k} = \left( 1 + \frac{i\, \Delta t_k^*}{t_k^* + \alpha_X} \right)^{\beta_X}.
\end{equation}
Since the time step $\Delta t_k = t_{k+1} - t_k$ is taken in the
self-similar time $t$, the corresponding physical increment must be obtained
from the clock,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta t_k^* = \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} \tau\, \mathrm{d} t,
\end{equation}
which can be approximated with a three-point Adams--Bashforth-style quadrature:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-dtstar}
  \Delta t_k^* = \int_{t_k}^{t_{k+1}} \tau\, \mathrm{d} t
  \approx w_0\, \tau_k + w_1\, \tau_{k-1} + w_2\, \tau_{k-2}.
\end{equation}
The coefficients can be computed as follows.
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-dtstar-weights}
  \begin{aligned}
    w_0 &= \frac{h^3/3 + (2h_1 + h_2)\, h^2/2 + h_1 (h_1 + h_2)\, h}{h_1 (h_1 + h_2)}, \\
    w_1 &= -\frac{h^3/3 + (h_1 + h_2)\, h^2/2}{h_1 h_2}, \\
    w_2 &= \frac{h^3/3 + h_1\, h^2/2}{h_2 (h_1 + h_2)},
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $h = \Delta t_k$, $h_1 = \Delta t_{k-1}$, $h_2 = \Delta t_{k-2}$.
\par
Eliminating $t_k^* + \alpha_X = X_k^{1/\beta_X}$ from
\eqref{eq:fw-powerlaw} and taking logarithms gives the function that the
algorithm uses in both directions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-G}
  \ln \frac{X_{k+i}}{X_k} = G_X(i, \beta_X; k),
  \qquad
  G_X(i, \beta; k) = \beta \ln\!\left( 1 + \exp\!\left( \ln i + \ln \Delta t_k^* - \frac{\ln X_k}{\beta} \right) \right).
\end{equation}
It is evaluated forward, with $i = \tfrac12$, to predict a half-step ratio
(\S\ref{ssec:alg-substeps}), and inverted for $\beta$, with $i = 1$, to
estimate the exponents from the realized history
(\S\ref{ssec:alg-beta-estimation}).

\paragraph{Logarithmic arithmetic.}
$U_k$ and $L_k$ may become extremely large or small, so we store
$\ln U_k$ and $\ln L_k$ rather than $U_k$ and $L_k$, and perform all
arithmetic on them in the logarithmic number system, where multiplication and
division become addition and subtraction, and where sums and differences are
evaluated by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-log-sum-exp}
  \log_b(X+Y) = \log_b(X) + \log_b\!\bigl(1+b^{\,y-x}\bigr),
  \qquad
  \log_b(X-Y) = \log_b(X) + \log_b\!\bigl(1-b^{\,y-x}\bigr) \quad (X>Y>0),
\end{equation}
with $x = \log_b(X)$, $y = \log_b(Y)$. In particular
\eqref{eq:fw-dtstar} is evaluated as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fw-ln-dtstar}
  \ln \Delta t_k^* =
  \ln\!\left( w_0\, e^{\ln \tau_k} + w_1\, e^{\ln \tau_{k-1}} + w_2\, e^{\ln \tau_{k-2}} \right).
\end{equation}

\section{Algorithm}\label{sec:algorithm}

The integrator below is stated once, for any equation that supplies the three
items of \S\ref{sec:framework}: a clock $\tau$, a physics operator
$\Phi^{(\mathrm{P})}$, and a pair of constraint functionals. Everything else
--- the splitting, the scale bookkeeping, the exponent estimation --- is
common.

\subsection{Domain, grid, and notation}\label{ssec:alg-domain}

The spatial domain is periodic with a fixed physical side length $H$ and $N$
grid points per direction, centred at the origin, so that
$x_j \in [-H/2, H/2)$ with uniform spacing $\Delta x = H/N$ and grid points
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alg-grid}
  x_n = -\frac{H}{2} + n\, \Delta x, \qquad n = 0, 1, \dots, N-1
\end{equation}
in each of the $d$ directions. Every integral over the domain below is
approximated by the Riemann sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alg-riemann}
  \int f(\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x} \approx (\Delta x)^d \sum_{\mathbf{n}} f(\mathbf{x_n})
\end{equation}
on this grid.

An index range abbreviates the corresponding tuple of stored values, e.g.\
$\ln \tau_{k-2:k} = (\ln \tau_{k-2}, \ln \tau_{k-1}, \ln \tau_k)$ and
$\Delta t_{k-2:k} = (\Delta t_{k-2}, \Delta t_{k-1}, \Delta t_k)$. The run
constants $H, N$, the time-step constants
$\Delta t_{\max}, C, \gamma, \Delta t_{\min}$ of \S\ref{ssec:alg-timestep}, the
constraint targets $F_1, F_2$, and whatever physical parameters the equation
carries are visible to every subroutine and are omitted from their signatures.
The step $\Delta t_k$ is updated dynamically. As set out in \S\ref{ssec:fw-powerlaw}, all
arithmetic on the scales is done on $\ln U_k, \ln L_k$.

\subsection{Initialization}\label{ssec:alg-init}

A run is specified by the initial field $u_0$, the domain parameters $H, N$,
the time-step constants $\Delta t_{\max}, C, \gamma, \Delta t_{\min}$ of
\S\ref{ssec:alg-timestep}, a time horizon $T_{\mathrm{end}}$, the
splitting scheme
$\mathit{scheme} \in \{\textsc{LieTrotter}, \textsc{Strang}\}$
(\S\ref{ssec:alg-lie-trotter}, \S\ref{ssec:alg-strang}), and initial guesses
$\beta_U^{(0)}, \beta_L^{(0)}$, which only seed the bracket of the first
root-find for $\beta_U, \beta_L$ (\S\ref{ssec:alg-beta-estimation}). From these:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The integration loop starts at $t_0 = 0$ and runs while $t_k < T_{\mathrm{end}}$.
  \item The self-similar frame is initialized at the initial condition itself,
  $L_0 = U_0 = 1$, i.e.\ $\ln L_0 = \ln U_0 = 0$.
  \item The \emph{target} values $F_1, F_2$ of the constraint functionals
  (\S\ref{ssec:fw-constraints}) are fixed once, from $u_0$ itself,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:alg-targets}
    F_1 = F_1[u_0], \qquad F_2 = F_2[u_0].
  \end{equation}
  \item The flag $\beta_{\mathrm{ok}}$, which records whether the current
  $\beta_U, \beta_L$ are usable by \textsc{Strang}, starts as
  $\beta_{\mathrm{ok}} = \text{false}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Time step}\label{ssec:alg-timestep}

The step $\Delta t_k$ is chosen at the start of each step from the field
$u_k$ it starts from,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alg-dt-rule}
  \Delta t_k = \min\bigl( \Delta t_{\max},\ \gamma\, \Delta t_{k-1},\
  \Delta t_{\mathrm{CFL}}(u_k; C) \bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t_{\mathrm{CFL}}(u; C) = \textsc{MaxStableDt}(u, C)$
(\S\ref{ssec:alg-substeps}) is the largest step at which the equation's
physics operator runs at the target CFL number $C$, expressed as a fraction of
the time integrator's own stability limit. For Navier--Stokes this is the
advective bound \eqref{eq:rk4-cfl-dt}. The three terms play distinct roles.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\Delta t_{\max}$ caps the step when the field is quiet, and is the
  step of the settled regime.
  \item $\Delta t_{\mathrm{CFL}}$ shrinks the step when the field concentrates:
  a momentary high-amplitude event raises $\max|\mathbf{u}|$ and would otherwise
  carry the physics step past its stability limit.
  \item $\gamma > 1$ caps \emph{growth only}. The step drops in a single step
  when an event arrives and recovers geometrically afterwards. Step $k = 0$ has
  no predecessor and is uncapped. The cap keeps the step ratios
  $h/h_1, h_1/h_2$ in the quadrature \eqref{eq:fw-dtstar-weights} moderate,
  and it limits the per-step growth of $L$, which drives the feedback between
  the predicted half step and the $\beta$ fit under \textsc{Strang}
  (\S\ref{ssec:alg-strang}). We take $C = 0.5$, $\gamma = 1.1$.
\end{itemize}
If \eqref{eq:alg-dt-rule} falls below $\Delta t_{\min}$ the run stops with an
error: a runaway $\max|\mathbf{u}|$ should end the run rather than stall it in
ever smaller steps. Fixed stepping at $\Delta t_{\max}$ is the special case
with no CFL term, $\Delta t_{\mathrm{CFL}} = \infty$. It is what the 1D
diffusion equation uses, since its propagator \eqref{eq:1d-propagator} is exact
at any step and has no CFL limit to adapt to.

\paragraph{No rejection.} Every step is accepted. The CFL is measured on
$u_k$, so the step overshoots its target only if $\max|\mathbf{u}|$ grows
within the step; with $C = 0.5$ it has to more than double in a single step
to breach the stability limit. Whether that happens is monitored rather than
prevented. The \emph{realized} CFL of step $k$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alg-realized-cfl}
  \mathrm{CFL}_k = \frac{\Delta t_k}{\Delta t_{\mathrm{CFL}}(u_{k+1}; 1)},
\end{equation}
measures the step actually taken against the field it produced. It is recorded
every step, and the run warns the first time it exceeds $1$. The pre-step value
would carry no information whenever the CFL term is active, since it reads
back $C$ by construction.

\paragraph{The end of the run.} The loop runs while $t_k < T_{\mathrm{end}}$,
with $t_{k+1} = t_k + \Delta t_k$, and the last step is \emph{not} shortened
to land on $T_{\mathrm{end}}$. A clipped step can be arbitrarily small, which
would put an extreme ratio $h/h_1$ into \eqref{eq:fw-dtstar-weights} on the
next $\beta$ update. The run overshoots $T_{\mathrm{end}}$ by less than one
step, which is immaterial on the logarithmic clock $t$. For the same reason,
output taken at prescribed times $t$ is taken at the first step reaching each
time, never by shortening a step.

\subsection{Top-level integrator}\label{ssec:alg-top-level}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Top-level integrator}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $u_0$, $H$, $N$, $\Delta t_{\max}, C, \gamma, \Delta t_{\min}$, $T_{\mathrm{end}}$, $\mathit{scheme}$, $\beta_U^{(0)}$, $\beta_L^{(0)}$
\State $t_0 \gets 0$, $k \gets 0$
\State $\ln U_0 \gets 0$, $\ln L_0 \gets 0$
\State Compute constraint targets $F_1, F_2$ from $u_0$ via \eqref{eq:alg-targets}
\State $\beta_U \gets \beta_U^{(0)}$, $\beta_L \gets \beta_L^{(0)}$, $\beta_{\mathrm{ok}} \gets \text{false}$
\State Store $(u_0, \ln U_0, \ln L_0, \ln \tau_0, \beta_U, \beta_L, \beta_{\mathrm{ok}})$
\While{$t_k < T_{\mathrm{end}}$}
  \State $\Delta t_k \gets$ \eqref{eq:alg-dt-rule} on $u_k$; \textbf{error} if $\Delta t_k < \Delta t_{\min}$ \Comment{\S\ref{ssec:alg-timestep}}
  \If{$\mathit{scheme} = \textsc{Strang}$ \textbf{and} $\beta_{\mathrm{ok}}$}
    \State $(u_{k+1}, \ln U_{k+1}, \ln L_{k+1}) \gets \Call{Strang}{u_k, \ln U_k, \ln L_k, \ln \tau_{k-2:k}, \Delta t_{k-2:k}, \beta_U, \beta_L}$ \Comment{\S\ref{ssec:alg-strang}}
  \Else
    \State $(u_{k+1}, \ln U_{k+1}, \ln L_{k+1}) \gets \Call{LieTrotter}{u_k, \ln U_k, \ln L_k, \Delta t_k}$ \Comment{\S\ref{ssec:alg-lie-trotter}}
  \EndIf
  \State $u_{k+1} \gets \Call{ApplyBC}{u_{k+1}}$ \Comment{equation-specific, \S\ref{ssec:alg-substeps}}
  \State $\ln \tau_{k+1} \gets \ln \tau(\ln U_{k+1}, \ln L_{k+1})$ \Comment{\eqref{eq:1d-ln-clock}, \eqref{eq:ns-ln-clock}}
  \State $t_{k+1} \gets t_k + \Delta t_k$
  \If{$k+1 \geq 3$}
    \State $(\beta_U, \beta_L, \beta_{\mathrm{ok}}) \gets \Call{EstimateBeta}{\ln U_{k:k+1}, \ln L_{k:k+1}, \ln \tau_{k-2:k}, \Delta t_{k-2:k}, \beta_U, \beta_L}$ \Comment{\S\ref{ssec:alg-beta-estimation}}
  \EndIf
  \State Store $(u_{k+1}, \ln U_{k+1}, \ln L_{k+1}, \ln \tau_{k+1}, t_{k+1}, \Delta t_k, \mathrm{CFL}_k, \beta_U, \beta_L, \beta_{\mathrm{ok}})$ \Comment{\eqref{eq:alg-realized-cfl}}
  \State $k \gets k + 1$
\EndWhile
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The run parameter $\mathit{scheme}$ selects the step function. \textsc{Strang}
needs usable estimates of $\beta_U, \beta_L$, so the loop falls back to
\textsc{LieTrotter} whenever $\beta_{\mathrm{ok}}$ is false. This is always
the case for the first three steps $k = 0, 1, 2$: the first $\beta$ update
needs four stored states, so it first runs at the end of step $k = 2$. It is
also the case for every step that follows a failed $\beta$ update
(\S\ref{ssec:alg-beta-estimation}), until a later update succeeds. The $\beta$
update itself runs regardless of $\mathit{scheme}$; under
$\mathit{scheme} = \textsc{LieTrotter}$ the estimates are diagnostic only.

\subsection{Substeps}\label{ssec:alg-substeps}
Both splitting schemes are assembled from the substeps below. None of them
applies the boundary treatment, which is handled only by the top-level loop.

\paragraph{\textsc{Evolve}$(u, \Delta t_k)$.}
Applies the physics operator $\Phi_{\Delta t_k}^{(\mathrm{P})}$ of
\eqref{eq:fw-physics-flow}: it advances the non-scaling terms of
\eqref{eq:fw-lemma} over $\Delta t_k$, leaving the scales $\ln U, \ln L$
untouched.

\paragraph{\textsc{MaxStableDt}$(u, C)$.}
Equation-specific: the largest step at which \textsc{Evolve} from $u$ runs at
the fraction $C$ of its stability limit, the term
$\Delta t_{\mathrm{CFL}}(u; C)$ of \eqref{eq:alg-dt-rule}. For Navier--Stokes
it is \eqref{eq:rk4-cfl-dt}. An equation without a CFL limit does not define
it and runs at fixed $\Delta t_{\max}$ (\S\ref{ssec:alg-timestep}).

\paragraph{\textsc{Scale}$(u, \ln \tilde U, \ln \tilde L)$.}
Applies the scaling operator \eqref{eq:fw-scale-step} with prescribed ratios
$\tilde U, \tilde L$, returning $\tilde U^{-1} u(\mathbf{x_n} \tilde L)$ at each grid
point, evaluated with the spectral interpolation of
\S\ref{ssec:fw-interpolation} and set to zero wherever $\mathbf{x_n}\tilde L$
leaves the box \eqref{eq:fw-dilate-cutoff}. Here, and wherever the substeps are used,
$\tilde U$ and $\tilde L$ denote the ratio of $U$, respectively $L$, after the
substep to its value before it. Inside a Lie--Trotter step this is
$\tilde U_{k+1} = U_{k+1}/U_k$ of \eqref{eq:fw-ratios}; inside a Strang step it
is a ratio across a half step, $U_{k+1/2}/U_k$ or $U_{k+1}/U_{k+1/2}$
(\S\ref{ssec:alg-strang}).

\paragraph{\textsc{ConstrainedScale}$(u, \ln U, \ln L)$.}
Rescales $u$ so that the result carries the fixed targets $F_1, F_2$ of
\eqref{eq:alg-targets}, and advances the log-scale state accordingly:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Functionals of the input,} $F_1^* = F_1[u]$, $F_2^* = F_2[u]$,
  evaluated on the grid via \eqref{eq:alg-riemann}.
  \item \textbf{Ratios} $\ln \tilde U, \ln \tilde L$ from the constraint system
  \eqref{eq:fw-constraint-system}.
  \item \textbf{Rescale,} $u' = \textsc{Scale}(u, \ln \tilde U, \ln \tilde L)$.
  \item \textbf{Return} $u'$ together with the advanced state
  $\ln U + \ln \tilde U$, $\ln L + \ln \tilde L$.
\end{enumerate}
No time increment enters \textsc{ConstrainedScale}: the ratios are fixed by the
functionals of the field it is given, not by the elapsed time. The same substep
therefore serves as the full scaling step of Lie--Trotter and as the closing
scaling half step of Strang.

\paragraph{\textsc{PredictScale}$(i, \ln U_k, \ln L_k, \ln \tau_{k-2:k}, \Delta t_{k-2:k}, \beta_U, \beta_L)$.}
Predicts the ratios over the fraction $i$ of step $k$ from the power-law form
\eqref{eq:fw-powerlaw} rather than from the constraints. It evaluates
$\ln \Delta t_k^*$ from \eqref{eq:fw-ln-dtstar}, with the weights
\eqref{eq:fw-dtstar-weights} of the steps $\Delta t_{k-2:k}$ --- the last of
which is the step about to be taken --- and returns
$\bigl(G_U(i, \beta_U; k),\, G_L(i, \beta_L; k)\bigr)$ using \eqref{eq:fw-G}.
The same function $G_X$ is inverted for $\beta$ in
\S\ref{ssec:alg-beta-estimation}.

\paragraph{\textsc{ApplyBC}$(u)$.}
Imposes whatever boundary treatment the equation requires, after the full step
has been assembled. Both equations treated here clamp the domain boundary to zero: the 1D
diffusion equation its two edge grid points (\S\ref{ssec:1d-boundary}), the
Navier--Stokes equations the six faces of the box
(\S\ref{ssec:ns-boundary}), optionally followed by a Leray projection back
onto the divergence-free fields \eqref{eq:ns-leray-bc}. For Navier--Stokes the
clamp is itself optional: with it disabled \textsc{ApplyBC} is the identity,
and the far-field condition is left to the sponge forcing of
\S\ref{ssec:ns-sponge}, which acts inside \textsc{Evolve}.

\subsection{\textsc{LieTrotter}: Lie--Trotter splitting}\label{ssec:alg-lie-trotter}
A Lie--Trotter step composes a full physics step with a full scaling step,
$u_{k+1} = \Phi_{\Delta t_k}^{(\mathrm{S})}\bigl(\Phi_{\Delta t_k}^{(\mathrm{P})}(u_k)\bigr)$,
with the scaling ratios fixed by the constraints. It uses no estimate of
$\beta_U, \beta_L$, so it is valid from the first step. It is written for a
general input state $(u, \ln U, \ln L)$ and step $\Delta t_k$ rather than only a stored state
$(u_k, \ln U_k, \ln L_k)$, so that \textsc{Strang} can call it on a
half-stepped state.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Lie--Trotter step}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{LieTrotter}{$u, \ln U, \ln L, \Delta t_k$}
  \State $u^{(\mathrm{P})} \gets \Call{Evolve}{u, \Delta t_k}$ \Comment{\S\ref{ssec:alg-substeps}}
  \State \Return $\Call{ConstrainedScale}{u^{(\mathrm{P})}, \ln U, \ln L}$ \Comment{\S\ref{ssec:alg-substeps}}
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{\textsc{Strang}: Strang splitting}\label{ssec:alg-strang}
A Strang step is the symmetric composition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alg-strang-composition}
  u_{k+1} = \Phi_{\Delta t_k/2}^{(\mathrm{S})}\Bigl(\Phi_{\Delta t_k}^{(\mathrm{P})}\bigl(\Phi_{\Delta t_k/2}^{(\mathrm{S})}(u_k)\bigr)\Bigr)
\end{equation}
of a scaling half step, a full physics step, and a second scaling half step.
The last two of these are exactly a Lie--Trotter step --- a full physics step
followed by a scaling step whose ratios come from the constraints --- so
\textsc{Strang} is a predicted scaling half step followed by a call to
\textsc{LieTrotter}.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Strang step}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Function{Strang}{$u_k, \ln U_k, \ln L_k, \ln \tau_{k-2:k}, \Delta t_{k-2:k}, \beta_U, \beta_L$}
  \State $\bigl(\ln (U_{k+1/2}/U_k),\, \ln (L_{k+1/2}/L_k)\bigr) \gets \Call{PredictScale}{\tfrac12, \ln U_k, \ln L_k, \ln \tau_{k-2:k}, \Delta t_{k-2:k}, \beta_U, \beta_L}$ \Comment{\eqref{eq:alg-strang-predict}}
  \State $u_{k+1/2} \gets \Call{Scale}{u_k, \ln (U_{k+1/2}/U_k), \ln (L_{k+1/2}/L_k)}$
  \State $\ln U_{k+1/2} \gets \ln U_k + \ln (U_{k+1/2}/U_k)$;\ \ $\ln L_{k+1/2} \gets \ln L_k + \ln (L_{k+1/2}/L_k)$
  \State \Return $\Call{LieTrotter}{u_{k+1/2}, \ln U_{k+1/2}, \ln L_{k+1/2}, \Delta t_k}$ \Comment{\S\ref{ssec:alg-lie-trotter}}
\EndFunction
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
No constraint fixes the state at $t_k + \Delta t_k/2$, so the first scaling half
step takes its ratios from the current power-law estimates, via
\textsc{PredictScale} with $i = 1/2$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alg-strang-predict}
  \ln\frac{U_{k+1/2}}{U_k} = G_U\!\left(\tfrac12, \beta_U; k\right), \qquad
  \ln\frac{L_{k+1/2}}{L_k} = G_L\!\left(\tfrac12, \beta_L; k\right).
\end{equation}
The call to \textsc{LieTrotter} then advances $u_{k+1/2}$ over the full
$\Delta t_k$, and its \textsc{ConstrainedScale} substep --- the second scaling
half step --- sets $U_{k+1}/U_{k+1/2}$ and $L_{k+1}/L_{k+1/2}$ so that
$u_{k+1}$ satisfies the constraints. The half-step state
$(u_{k+1/2}, \ln U_{k+1/2}, \ln L_{k+1/2})$ is internal to the step and is not
stored. The top-level loop calls \textsc{Strang} only when
$\beta_{\mathrm{ok}}$ is true (\S\ref{ssec:alg-top-level}), which guarantees
that $\beta_U, \beta_L$ were fitted to the stored history at the end of the
previous step and that $\ln \tau_{k-2}$ exists.

\subsection{\textsc{EstimateBeta}: estimating \texorpdfstring{$\beta_U$}{beta\_U} and \texorpdfstring{$\beta_L$}{beta\_L}}\label{ssec:alg-beta-estimation}
After every step with $k+1 \geq 3$, regardless of $\mathit{scheme}$, $\beta_U$
and $\beta_L$ are re-estimated from the log-scale history. Four stored states
are needed: $\ln X_k$ and $\ln X_{k+1}$ for the ratio, and $\ln \tau_{k-2:k}$
with the steps $\Delta t_{k-2:k}$ for $\Delta t_k^*$. The update inverts the
function $G_X$ of \eqref{eq:fw-G} with $i = 1$, the same function that
\textsc{PredictScale} evaluates forward with $i = 1/2$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:alg-beta-rootfind}
  G_U(1, \beta_U; k) = \ln U_{k+1} - \ln U_k,
\end{equation}
and identically with $U \to L$ for $\beta_L$. The steps $\Delta t_{k-2:k}$
end in the step just taken, so the $\Delta t_k^*$ inverted here is the one
\textsc{PredictScale} used for that step's half step: under a varying step,
prediction and estimation remain exact inverses on the same step.

The sensitivity of $\beta_X$ to an error in $\Delta t_k^*$ is weak. Taking
logarithms of \eqref{eq:fw-G} for a small step,
$\ln(\ln X_{k+1} - \ln X_k) \approx \ln \beta_X + \ln \Delta t_k^* - \ln X_k/\beta_X$,
so a relative error $\epsilon$ in $\Delta t_k^*$ moves $\beta_X$ by
$\delta\beta_X \approx \beta_X^2 \epsilon / \ln X_k$, damped as the scales grow.
It is not zero, however: feeding a stale step into \eqref{eq:fw-dtstar-weights}
instead of $\Delta t_k$ makes the estimate oscillate with the step sequence.

Each of
\eqref{eq:alg-beta-rootfind} and its $L$-counterpart is a single scalar
equation in a single unknown ($\beta_U$, resp.\ $\beta_L$), solved by
\texttt{brentq}; the implementer will need to choose a bracketing interval
(e.g.\ centred on the previous $\beta_U, \beta_L$ estimate --- on
$\beta_U^{(0)}, \beta_L^{(0)}$ for the first update --- widened adaptively if
it fails to bracket a root).

If both root-finds succeed, \textsc{EstimateBeta} returns the new
$\beta_U, \beta_L$ with $\beta_{\mathrm{ok}} = \text{true}$. If either fails
to bracket a root even after widening, the failed estimate keeps its previous
value (which seeds the next bracket), and \textsc{EstimateBeta} returns
$\beta_{\mathrm{ok}} = \text{false}$, so the next step falls back to
\textsc{LieTrotter}. Before $k+1 \geq 3$ no update is made: $\beta_U, \beta_L$
stay at $\beta_U^{(0)}, \beta_L^{(0)}$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{ok}}$ stays false.

\section{Post-processing}\label{sec:postproc}

A completed run leaves behind the log-scale history $\ln U_k, \ln L_k$ and the
clock history $\ln \tau_k$. From these we recover the power-law parameters
$\alpha_X, \beta_X$ of the ansatz \eqref{eq:fw-powerlaw}. This is the offline,
global counterpart of \textsc{EstimateBeta} (\S\ref{ssec:alg-beta-estimation}),
which fits $\beta_X$ one step at a time; here the whole history is used at
once, so the fit also serves as a check on the run.

Write
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-logs}
  \mathcal{L}_k = \ln L_k, \qquad \mathcal{U}_k = \ln U_k,
\end{equation}
together with the per-step log-ratios
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-log-ratios}
  \ell_k = \ln L_{k+1} - \ln L_k, \qquad \lambda_k = \ln U_{k+1} - \ln U_k,
\end{equation}
which for a Lie--Trotter step equal $\ln \tilde{L}_{k+1}$ and
$\ln \tilde{U}_{k+1}$ of \eqref{eq:fw-ratios}, and for a Strang step combine
both half-step ratios. The stored scales are their cumulative sums,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-cumsum}
  \mathcal{L}_k = \mathcal{L}_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \ell_i,
  \qquad
  \mathcal{U}_k = \mathcal{U}_0 + \sum_{i=1}^{k} \lambda_i,
\end{equation}
which grow only linearly in $k$; unlike $L_k$ and $U_k$ themselves,
$\mathcal{L}_k$ and $\mathcal{U}_k$ never overflow.

\subsection{The scaling rates under the ansatz}\label{ssec:pp-rates}

Differentiating the ansatz \eqref{eq:fw-powerlaw} with respect to $t$, and
using the clock \eqref{eq:fw-clock} to convert from $t^*$, the logarithmic
rates \eqref{eq:fw-rates} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-rate-chain}
  \sigma_X = \frac{1}{X}\frac{\mathrm{d} X}{\mathrm{d} t^*}\frac{\mathrm{d} t^*}{\mathrm{d} t}
  = \frac{\beta_X\, \tau}{t^* + \alpha_X},
  \qquad X \in \{U, L\}.
\end{equation}
Inverting the ansatz to eliminate the unknown offset,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-tstar-offset}
  t^* + \alpha_X = X^{1/\beta_X},
\end{equation}
and substituting into \eqref{eq:pp-rate-chain} gives the rates in terms of the
scales alone,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-rate}
  \sigma_X = \beta_X\, \tau\, X^{-1/\beta_X}.
\end{equation}
Taking logarithms,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-rate-log}
  \ln \sigma_{X,k} = \ln \beta_X + \ln \tau_k - \frac{\mathcal{X}_k}{\beta_X},
  \qquad \mathcal{X}_k = \ln X_k .
\end{equation}
Finally, the rates themselves are estimated directly from the log-ratios
already in hand, without any finite difference of $L_k$ or $U_k$ themselves,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-rate-fd}
  \sigma_{L,k} \approx \frac{\ell_k}{\Delta t_k},
  \qquad
  \sigma_{U,k} \approx \frac{\lambda_k}{\Delta t_k},
\end{equation}
each divided by its own step, so that a run with a varying step
(\S\ref{ssec:alg-timestep}) is fitted exactly as a fixed-step one. The rates,
not the log-ratios $\ell_k, \lambda_k$ themselves, are also what the run's
diagnostics plot: a ratio scales with its step, so a CFL-driven dip in
$\Delta t_k$ would read as a dip in the scaling.

\subsection{Recovering \texorpdfstring{$\alpha_X$}{alpha\_X} and \texorpdfstring{$\beta_X$}{beta\_X}}\label{ssec:pp-fit}

Equation \eqref{eq:pp-rate-log} is linear in $\mathcal{X}_k$ once the clock is
divided out. Regressing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-regression}
  \ln \sigma_{X,k} - \ln \tau_k = \ln \beta_X - \frac{1}{\beta_X}\, \mathcal{X}_k
\end{equation}
against $\mathcal{X}_k$ therefore yields $\beta_X$ twice over: once as
$e^{\text{intercept}}$ and once as $-1/\text{slope}$. Agreement between the two
is a check that the run is in fact following a power law over the fitted range;
disagreement indicates that it is not, or that the range includes the initial
transient before the frame has locked on.

With $\beta_X$ in hand, the offset follows from \eqref{eq:pp-tstar-offset},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-alpha}
  \alpha_X = X_k^{1/\beta_X} - t_k^*,
\end{equation}
where the physical time is accumulated from the per-step increments of
\eqref{eq:fw-dtstar}, with the weights \eqref{eq:fw-dtstar-weights} of the
stored steps (the first two steps, which lack a three-point history, take
$\Delta t_i^* \approx \Delta t_i \tau_i$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-tstar-cumsum}
  t_k^* = t_0^* + \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \Delta t_i^* .
\end{equation}
In exact arithmetic \eqref{eq:pp-alpha} is independent of $k$; in practice it
is averaged over the fitted range, and its scatter is a second check on the
ansatz.

For the 1D diffusion equation the clock is $\tau = L^2/D$, and
\eqref{eq:pp-rate-log} specializes to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-1d-sigma-L}
  \ln \sigma_{L,k} = \ln \frac{\beta_L}{D} + \left( 2 - \frac{1}{\beta_L} \right) \mathcal{L}_k,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pp-1d-sigma-U}
  \ln \sigma_{U,k} = \ln \frac{\beta_U}{D} + 2\, \mathcal{L}_k - \frac{\mathcal{U}_k}{\beta_U},
\end{equation}
so that for $X = L$ the regression \eqref{eq:pp-regression} collapses onto a
single variable, while for $X = U$ it is a two-variable fit in
$\mathcal{L}_k$ and $\mathcal{U}_k$.

\section{The 1D diffusion equation}\label{sec:1d}

The first instantiation of the framework is the one-dimensional diffusion
equation, $d = 1$, with a single scalar component; we drop the component index
throughout this section. It serves as a testbed: every operator the framework
needs is available in closed form, so the only approximation in a run is the
operator splitting itself.

\subsection{Governing equation and clock}\label{ssec:1d-clock}

The dimensional equation is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-dimensional}
  \frac{\partial u^*}{\partial t^*} = D \frac{\partial^2 u^*}{\partial (x^*)^2},
\end{equation}
so that, in the notation of \eqref{eq:fw-general-pde},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-R}
  R^*(u^*) = D \frac{\partial^2 u^*}{\partial (x^*)^2}.
\end{equation}
Under the rescaling \eqref{eq:fw-nondim} this term becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-R-rescaled}
  D \frac{\partial^2 (u U)}{\partial (x^*)^2}
  = D U \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left( \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} \frac{1}{L} \right) \frac{1}{L}
  = \frac{D U}{L^2} \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2},
\end{equation}
having used $\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}x^* = 1/L$. The clock is then chosen to make
the physical term of the lemma \eqref{eq:fw-lemma} carry unit coefficient,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-clock}
  \frac{\tau}{U} \cdot \frac{D U}{L^2} = 1
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \tau = \frac{L^2}{D},
\end{equation}
which is the diffusive time scale of the current length scale, as expected.
In log form, as required by \eqref{eq:fw-ln-dtstar},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-ln-clock}
  \ln \tau_k = 2 \ln L_k - \ln D .
\end{equation}
Substituting \eqref{eq:1d-clock} into the rescaling lemma
\eqref{eq:fw-lemma} gives the equation the algorithm integrates,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-transformed}
  \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}
  = \sigma_L\, x \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}
  - \sigma_U\, u
  + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The physics step}\label{ssec:1d-physics-step}

Because the diffusion term in \eqref{eq:1d-transformed} has unit coefficient,
the physics operator \eqref{eq:fw-physics-flow},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-physics-flow}
  \Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{P})}(u_k)
  = u_k + \int_{t_k}^{t_k + \Delta t} \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}\, \mathrm{d}t,
\end{equation}
has the closed-form spectral solution
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-propagator}
  \hat u_j^{(\mathrm{P})} = \hat u_j\, e^{-k_j^2 \Delta t},
  \qquad k_j = \frac{2\pi j}{H}, \quad j = 0, \dots, \left\lfloor N/2 \right\rfloor,
\end{equation}
where $\hat u_j = \mathrm{RFFT}(u)_j$. This is an exact one-shot spectral
propagator, not an approximate time-marching scheme, so no sub-stepping or
implicit solve is needed, and the accuracy requirement on \textsc{Evolve}
(\S\ref{ssec:alg-substeps}) is met trivially at any order. Inverse-transforming
gives the advanced field $u^{(\mathrm{P})}(x_n)$.

\subsection{Moment constraints}\label{ssec:1d-moments}

The constraint functionals of \S\ref{ssec:fw-constraints} are taken to be the
zeroth and second moments of the field,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-functionals}
  F_1[u] = \int u\, \mathrm{d}x \equiv M_0,
  \qquad
  F_2[u] = \int x^2 u\, \mathrm{d}x \equiv M_2,
\end{equation}
so that $M_0$ measures the amount of the conserved quantity and $M_2$ its
spread. Writing $M_0^*, M_2^*$ for the moments of the field being scaled, and
substituting $y = x\, \tilde L$ in each integral, the scaled field carries
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-M0-homogeneity}
  \int \frac{1}{\tilde U} u\!\left( x\, \tilde L \right) \mathrm{d}x
  = \frac{1}{\tilde U \tilde L}\, M_0^*,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-M2-homogeneity}
  \int x^2\, \frac{1}{\tilde U} u\!\left( x\, \tilde L \right) \mathrm{d}x
  = \frac{1}{\tilde U \tilde L^{3}}\, M_2^*,
\end{equation}
so the homogeneity exponents of \eqref{eq:fw-homogeneity} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-exponents}
  (p_1, q_1) = (1, 1), \qquad (p_2, q_2) = (1, 3),
\end{equation}
with $p_1 q_2 - p_2 q_1 = 2 \neq 0$, so the constraint system
\eqref{eq:fw-constraint-system} is uniquely solvable: the two moments do
respond independently to amplitude and to length rescaling. Solving it,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-Ltilde}
  \tilde L = \sqrt{\frac{M_0\, M_2^*}{M_0^*\, M_2}},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-Utilde}
  \tilde U = \frac{M_0^*}{M_0\, \tilde L}
  = \left(\frac{M_0^*}{M_0}\right)^{3/2} \sqrt{\frac{M_2}{M_2^*}} .
\end{equation}

\emph{Open.} An earlier form of \eqref{eq:1d-Ltilde} and \eqref{eq:1d-Utilde}
omitted the factor $M_0$, giving
$\tilde L = \sqrt{M_2^*/(M_0^* M_2)}$ and
$\tilde U = (M_0^*)^{3/2} M_0^{-1} \sqrt{M_2/M_2^*}$; the present
implementation uses that form. The two agree exactly when $M_0 = 1$, and
differ by a factor $M_0^{\pm 1/2}$ otherwise. The discrepancy traces to
whether the target second moment is taken raw, as in \eqref{eq:1d-functionals}
and \eqref{eq:alg-targets}, or normalized by $M_0$. Since diffusion conserves
$M_0$ and the initial condition is in practice normalized to unit mass, the
difference has not so far been visible in a run. Which convention is intended
should be settled, and the implementation brought into line with it.

\subsection{Spectral interpolation on the real transform}\label{ssec:1d-interpolation}

For $d = 1$ and a real-valued field, the generic interpolation formula
\eqref{eq:fw-spectral-interp} is realized on the non-negative frequency
components of a real Fast Fourier Transform ($\mathrm{RFFT}$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-interp}
  \begin{split}
    \Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{S})}(u_k)(x) = \frac{1}{\tilde{U}_{k+1}}\biggl\{ &\hat{u}_0^{(k)}
      + 2 \sum_{j=1}^{N/2-1} (-1)^j \biggl[ \operatorname{Re}\bigl(\hat{u}_j^{(k)}\bigr) \cos\left(\frac{2\pi j \tilde{L}_{k+1} x}{H}\right) \\
      &\qquad - \operatorname{Im}\bigl(\hat{u}_j^{(k)}\bigr) \sin\left(\frac{2\pi j \tilde{L}_{k+1} x}{H}\right) \biggr] \\
      &+ (-1)^{N/2}\, \hat{u}_{N/2}^{(k)} \cos\left(\frac{\pi N \tilde{L}_{k+1} x}{H}\right) \biggr\},
  \end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{u}_j^{(k)} = \frac{1}{N} \mathrm{RFFT}(u_k)_j$ for
$j = 0, 1, \dots, N/2$, and the Nyquist term at $j = N/2$ is included only if
$N$ is even. The factor of $2$ accounts for the Hermitian conjugate modes
omitted by the real transform. \eqref{eq:1d-interp} holds for
$|\tilde L_{k+1} x| \le H/2$; outside that the cutoff
\eqref{eq:fw-dilate-cutoff} sets $\Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{S})}(u_k)(x) = 0$.

The alternating sign $(-1)^j$ is the phase-origin correction, and it is not
optional. The transform phases its coefficients to the sample index $n$, whose
origin $n = 0$ sits at $x = -H/2$ on the centred grid \eqref{eq:alg-grid}
rather than at $x = 0$. Since the scaling operator \eqref{eq:fw-scale-step}
dilates about the centre of the domain, each mode must be evaluated at
$\tilde L_{k+1} x + H/2$ --- dilate about $x = 0$, then shift to the
transform's phase origin --- and not at $\tilde L_{k+1}(x + H/2)$, which
dilates the shift along with the coordinate and agrees with it only at
$\tilde L_{k+1} = 1$. As $H/2$ is half a period, that shift contributes the
constant phase $\exp(\mathrm{i} k_j H / 2) = (-1)^j$. Dropping it altogether
returns $u_k\!\left(\tilde L_{k+1} x - H/2\right)$, the wanted profile
displaced by half the domain, rather than the
$u_k\!\left(\tilde L_{k+1} x\right)$ that \eqref{eq:fw-scale-step} calls for.
The same factor carries to each axis in three dimensions
(\S\ref{ssec:ns-interpolation}).

\subsection{Boundary treatment}\label{ssec:1d-boundary}

The substep \textsc{ApplyBC} of \S\ref{ssec:alg-substeps} imposes a homogeneous
Dirichlet condition by clamping the two edge grid points of the centred domain
\eqref{eq:alg-grid} after each completed step,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:1d-clamp}
  u_{k+1}(x_0) \gets 0, \qquad u_{k+1}(x_{N-1}) \gets 0 .
\end{equation}
On the centred grid $x \in [-H/2, H/2)$ these are two distinct grid points, not
periodic images of one another.

\emph{Open.} This sits in tension with the rest of the discretization: the
spectral propagator \eqref{eq:1d-propagator} presumes a periodic field, whereas
\eqref{eq:1d-clamp} imposes a Dirichlet condition. The spectral interpolation
\eqref{eq:1d-interp} no longer does at the edges: with the cutoff
\eqref{eq:fw-dilate-cutoff} it treats everything beyond the box as zero, which
is consistent with the clamp, but it is still a periodic interpolant inside the
box. The two are consistent only
so long as $u$ is negligible near the domain edges, which requires the initial
condition to be localized well inside the domain and the run to be stopped
before the profile spreads to the boundary. The scaling step works against
this --- it is what keeps the profile from spreading in the self-similar frame
--- but the condition is an assumption of the scheme rather than a consequence
of it, and is not currently monitored.

\section{The Navier--Stokes equations}\label{sec:ns}

The second instantiation is the incompressible Navier--Stokes equations, with
$d = 3$ and $u_i$ the velocity field. The framework applies unchanged; what
this section supplies is the clock, the nondimensional right-hand side, the
structure of the physics operator, and the constraint functionals. One item
needed to actually run the algorithm of \S\ref{sec:algorithm} --- the
time-marching scheme inside $\Phi^{(\mathrm{P})}$ --- remains open, and is
marked as such below.

\subsection{Governing equations and clock}\label{ssec:ns-clock}

The equations are solved as a large-eddy simulation (LES): $u_i^*$ and $p^*$
denote the filtered (resolved) velocity and pressure, and the effect of the
unresolved scales enters through the kinematic subgrid-scale stress
$\tau_{ij}^*$. The dimensional filtered equations are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-momentum}
  \frac{\partial u_i^*}{\partial t^*} + u_j^* \frac{\partial u_i^*}{\partial x_j^*}= -\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial p^*}{\partial x_i^*} + \nu \frac{\partial^2 u_i^*}{\partial x_j^* \partial x_j^*} - \frac{\partial \tau_{ij}^*}{\partial x_j^*},
\end{equation}
together with the continuity equation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-continuity}
  \frac{\partial u_i^*}{\partial x_i^*} = 0,
\end{equation}
so that, in the notation of \eqref{eq:fw-general-pde},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-R}
  R_i^*(u^*) = - u_j^* \frac{\partial u_i^*}{\partial x_j^*}
  - \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial p^*}{\partial x_i^*}
  + \nu \frac{\partial^2 u_i^*}{\partial x_j^* \partial x_j^*}
  - \frac{\partial \tau_{ij}^*}{\partial x_j^*}.
\end{equation}
In addition to the rescaling \eqref{eq:fw-nondim} of $u_i$ and $x_j$, the
pressure is nondimensionalized by the dynamic pressure,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-pressure-nondim}
  p = \frac{p^*}{\rho U^2},
\end{equation}
and the subgrid-scale stress, which carries units of velocity squared, by
$U^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-sgs-nondim}
  \tau_{ij} = \frac{\tau_{ij}^*}{U^2}.
\end{equation}
Term by term, the convective, pressure, and subgrid-scale terms scale as
$U^2/L$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-convection}
  u_j^* \frac{\partial u_i^*}{\partial x_j^*} = \frac{U^2}{L} u_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j},
  \qquad
  \frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial p^*}{\partial x_i^*} = \frac{U^2}{L} \frac{\partial p}{\partial x_i},
  \qquad
  \frac{\partial \tau_{ij}^*}{\partial x_j^*} = \frac{U^2}{L} \frac{\partial \tau_{ij}}{\partial x_j},
\end{equation}
while the viscous term scales as $\nu U / L^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-viscous}
  \nu \frac{\partial^2 u_i^*}{\partial x_j^* \partial x_j^*} = \frac{\nu U}{L^2}\frac{\partial^2 u_i}{\partial x_j \partial x_j}.
\end{equation}
The clock is chosen so that the dominant, advective part of the physical term
of the lemma \eqref{eq:fw-lemma} carries unit coefficient,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-clock}
  \frac{\tau}{U} \cdot \frac{U^2}{L} = 1
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \tau = \frac{L}{U},
\end{equation}
which is the eddy turnover time of the current scales. In log form, as
required by \eqref{eq:fw-ln-dtstar},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-ln-clock}
  \ln \tau_k = \ln L_k - \ln U_k .
\end{equation}
Applying $\tau / U = L/U^2$ to \eqref{eq:ns-R} and introducing the
instantaneous Reynolds number
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-reynolds}
  Re = \frac{U L}{\nu},
\end{equation}
the rescaling lemma \eqref{eq:fw-lemma} gives the nondimensional momentum
equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-nondim-momentum-base}
  \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t}
  = \sigma_L\, x_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}
  - \sigma_U\, u_i
  - u_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}
  - \frac{\partial p}{\partial x_i}
  + \frac{1}{Re} \frac{\partial^2 u_i}{\partial x_j \partial x_j}
  - \frac{\partial \tau_{ij}}{\partial x_j},
\end{equation}
and the continuity equation is unchanged by the rescaling,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-nondim-continuity}
  \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_i} = 0.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Subgrid stress model}\label{ssec:ns-viscosity}
The subgrid-scale stress $\tau_{ij}^*$ of \eqref{eq:ns-momentum} is closed with
the standard Smagorinsky model. Its deviatoric part is taken proportional to the
resolved strain rate,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-smagorinsky}
  \tau_{ij}^* - \frac{1}{3}\tau_{kk}^*\delta_{ij} = -2\nu_{\mathrm{t}}^* S_{ij}^*,
  \qquad
  S_{ij}^* = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial u_i^*}{\partial x_j^*} + \frac{\partial u_j^*}{\partial x_i^*}\right),
\end{equation}
with the isotropic part $\frac{1}{3}\tau_{kk}^*\delta_{ij}$ absorbed into the
pressure, and the eddy viscosity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-eddy-viscosity}
  \nu_{\mathrm{t}}^* = (C_s \Delta^*)^2 |S^*|,
  \qquad
  |S^*| = \sqrt{2 S_{ij}^* S_{ij}^*},
\end{equation}
where $C_s$ is the Smagorinsky constant and $\Delta^*$ is the filter width.
Nondimensionalizing the filter width with the length scale, $\Delta = \Delta^*/L$,
which we take equal to the grid spacing, $\Delta = \Delta x$, and noting that
$S_{ij}^* = (U/L)\, S_{ij}$, the eddy viscosity becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-eddy-viscosity-nondim}
  \nu_{\mathrm{t}}^* = U L\, (C_s \Delta)^2 |S|,
\end{equation}
so that the nondimensional subgrid stress \eqref{eq:ns-sgs-nondim} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-smagorinsky-nondim}
  \tau_{ij} - \frac{1}{3}\tau_{kk}\delta_{ij}
  = -2 (C_s \Delta)^2 |S|\, S_{ij}.
\end{equation}
The stress carries no dependence on $U$ or $L$: the Smagorinsky model is
compatible with the self-similar rescaling. In the limit of infinite Reynolds
number, $1/Re = 0$, and the momentum equation
\eqref{eq:ns-nondim-momentum-base} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-nondim-momentum}
  \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t}
  = \sigma_L\, x_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}
  - \sigma_U\, u_i
  - u_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}
  - \frac{\partial p}{\partial x_i}
  + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[ 2 (C_s \Delta)^2 |S|\, S_{ij} \right],
\end{equation}
where $p$ now denotes the modified pressure that includes
$\frac{1}{3}\tau_{kk}$.

\subsection{Sponge forcing}\label{ssec:ns-sponge}
The condition we want at the edge of the domain is that of an unbounded,
quiescent far field, $u_i \to 0$ as $r \to \infty$, with $r = \sqrt{x_j x_j}$
the distance from the centre of the domain \eqref{eq:alg-grid}. The
pseudo-spectral discretization, however, presumes a periodic field, and cannot
impose that condition directly. We approximate it instead by a sponge (fringe)
layer: a forcing that damps the velocity towards zero in a spherical shell
near the edge of the box and vanishes in its interior,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-sponge-force}
  f_i = -W(r)\, u_i .
\end{equation}
The layer is meant to absorb whatever reaches it without reflecting it. It is
not a Brinkman penalization of a solid wall: $W$ ramps up smoothly to a
moderate value rather than jumping to a stiff one, and no no-slip condition is
imposed at any radius.

\paragraph{The weight function.} The weight is the fringe function of
Nordstr\"om, Nordin \& Henningson (1999), applied radially,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-sponge-W}
  W(r) = W_{\max}\, S\!\left( \frac{r - r_0}{r_1 - r_0} \right),
\end{equation}
where the smooth step $S$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-sponge-S}
  S(\xi) =
  \begin{cases}
    0, & \xi \le 0, \\[4pt]
    \dfrac{1}{1 + \exp\!\left( \dfrac{1}{\xi - 1} + \dfrac{1}{\xi} \right)}, & 0 < \xi < 1, \\[10pt]
    1, & \xi \ge 1 .
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
The function $S$ is $C^\infty$ on the whole real line. Every derivative of it
vanishes at $\xi = 0$ and at $\xi = 1$, it rises monotonically between them,
and it is antisymmetric about its midpoint, $S(1 - \xi) = 1 - S(\xi)$, so that
$S(1/2) = 1/2$. The weight \eqref{eq:ns-sponge-W} is therefore exactly zero in
the sphere $r \le r_0$, rises smoothly across the shell $r_0 < r < r_1$, and
is exactly $W_{\max}$ for $r \ge r_1$. It has three parameters:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $W_{\max}$, the damping rate in the fully developed layer, in units of
  $1/\tau$, i.e.\ per eddy turnover time \eqref{eq:ns-clock}. Default
  $W_{\max} = 10$, which damps the velocity by a factor $e$ in a tenth of a
  turnover. Setting $W_{\max} = 0$ switches the sponge off.
  \item $r_0$, the radius at which the ramp begins. Default $r_0 = 0.35\, H$.
  \item $r_1$, the radius at which the ramp ends. Default $r_1 = H/2$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Smoothness.} The sponge must not introduce Gibbs oscillations into
the pseudo-spectral evaluation of $W u_i$, so $W$ must be smooth not only on
the box but on its periodic extension. With $r_1 \le H/2$ the sphere
$r \le r_1$ lies inside the box, and $W = W_{\max}$ on every face and corner.
At $r_1 = H/2$ the sphere touches the centre of each face, where the periodic
image just across the face lies at $r$ slightly below $H/2$; since every
derivative of $S$ vanishes at $\xi = 1$, the periodic extension of $W$ is still
$C^\infty$ there. The default $r_1 = H/2$ is thus the largest admissible value,
and it leaves the largest interior. The ramp $S$ climbs more steeply than its
nominal width $r_1 - r_0$ suggests, so that width must be resolved by several
grid cells: we require $r_1 - r_0 \gtrsim 10\, \Delta x$. The defaults give
$r_1 - r_0 = 0.15\, H$, about $19\, \Delta x$ at $N = 128$ and about
$10\, \Delta x$ at $N = 64$.

\paragraph{The sponge in the self-similar frame.} The weight
\eqref{eq:ns-sponge-W} is fixed in the nondimensional coordinates $x_j$, and
$W$ is a rate in units of $1/\tau$. In physical variables
\eqref{eq:ns-sponge-force} is therefore the forcing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-sponge-dimensional}
  f_i^* = -\frac{U}{L}\, W\!\left( \frac{r^*}{L} \right) u_i^*,
\end{equation}
since $f_i^* = -(U^2/L)\, W(r)\, u_i$ and the lemma \eqref{eq:fw-lemma}
multiplies it by $\tau/U = L/U^2$, recovering \eqref{eq:ns-sponge-force}. In
physical space the layer thus moves outwards with the length scale $L$, and
its damping rate follows the eddy turnover rate $U/L$. Like the
Smagorinsky stress, the sponge carries no explicit dependence on $U$ or $L$ in
the self-similar frame.

\paragraph{The forced equation.} Adding \eqref{eq:ns-sponge-force} to
\eqref{eq:ns-nondim-momentum}, the momentum equation actually solved is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-nondim-momentum-sponge}
  \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial t}
  = \sigma_L\, x_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}
  - \sigma_U\, u_i
  - u_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}
  - \frac{\partial p}{\partial x_i}
  + \frac{\partial}{\partial x_j}\left[ 2 (C_s \Delta)^2 |S|\, S_{ij} \right]
  - W(r)\, u_i .
\end{equation}
The forcing is not divergence free: with $\partial_i u_i = 0$,
$\partial_i (W u_i) = u_i\, \partial_i W$, which is nonzero wherever the flow
crosses the ramp. It is not required to be. It enters the pressure Poisson
equation alongside the convective and subgrid stress terms
(\S\ref{ssec:ns-projection}), and the solenoidal projection then removes its
gradient part, so the velocity update stays divergence free. The projected
forcing is no longer strictly confined to the shell --- its gradient part is
taken up by the pressure, which is nonlocal --- but it remains purely
dissipative: $P_{il}$ is an orthogonal projection and $u_i$ is solenoidal, so
the rate at which it changes $E_0$ of \eqref{eq:ns-functionals} is
$-2 \int W\, u_i u_i\, \mathrm{d}\mathbf{x} \le 0$.

\paragraph{Initial condition.} The puff must start well inside the undamped
sphere $r \le r_0$, or the sponge will act on it from the first step. With the
window \eqref{eq:ns-ic-window} of width $\sigma$, we take
$\sigma \lesssim r_0 / 4$ as a guideline; it is not enforced.

\paragraph{Scaling drift.} The scaling term $\sigma_L x_j \partial_j u_i$ of
\eqref{eq:ns-nondim-momentum-sponge} is an advection with velocity
$-\sigma_L \mathbf{x}$. For $\sigma_L > 0$ it points inwards, so the
self-similar frame itself carries content away from the sponge, and the layer
has only to absorb what the turbulence carries outwards against that drift.

\subsection{Fourier representation}\label{ssec:ns-fourier}
Using the Fourier convention \eqref{eq:fw-fourier-convention}, the pressure
gradient becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-fourier-pressure}
  \widehat{\frac{\partial p}{\partial x_i}} = \mathrm{i} k_i \hat{p}.
\end{equation}
The convective term
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-fourier-convection}
  \hat{N}_i = \widehat{u_j \frac{\partial u_i}{\partial x_j}},
\end{equation}
will be evaluated pseudo-spectrally, and so will the subgrid stress term.
Writing
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-eddy-viscosity-field}
  \nu_{\mathrm{t}} = (C_s \Delta)^2 |S|
\end{equation}
for the nondimensional eddy viscosity of \eqref{eq:ns-eddy-viscosity-nondim},
the strain rate is formed spectrally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-fourier-strain}
  \hat{S}_{ij} = \frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\left( k_j \hat{u}_i + k_i \hat{u}_j \right),
\end{equation}
the products $|S|$ and $T_{ij} = 2 \nu_{\mathrm{t}} S_{ij}$ are formed in physical
space, and the divergence is taken spectrally,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-fourier-sgs}
  \hat{G}_i = \widehat{\frac{\partial T_{ij}}{\partial x_j}} = \mathrm{i} k_j \hat{T}_{ij}.
\end{equation}
Since $|S|$ is not a polynomial in $u$, $\hat{G}_i$ carries aliasing that the
$2/3$ rule does not remove entirely. The sponge forcing
\eqref{eq:ns-sponge-force} is likewise formed as a product in physical space
and transformed,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-fourier-sponge}
  \hat{F}_i = \widehat{W u_i},
\end{equation}
with $W$ tabulated once on the grid \eqref{eq:alg-grid}. Because $W$ is smooth
but not band-limited, $\hat{F}_i$ also carries content beyond the retained
band, which the $2/3$ mask removes along with that of $\hat{N}_i$ and
$\hat{G}_i$. The scaling terms transform as in
\eqref{eq:fw-scaling-pde-fourier} with $d = 3$, so the transform of
\eqref{eq:ns-nondim-momentum-sponge} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-nondim-momentum-fourier}
  \frac{\partial \hat{u}_i}{\partial t}
  = -\sigma_L \left( k_j \frac{\partial \hat{u}_i}{\partial k_j} + 3\, \hat{u}_i \right)
  - \sigma_U\, \hat{u}_i
  - \hat{N}_i
  - \mathrm{i} k_i \hat{p}
  + \hat{G}_i
  - \hat{F}_i,
\end{equation}
and the transform of the continuity equation
\eqref{eq:ns-nondim-continuity} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-fourier-continuity}
  k_i \hat{u}_i = 0.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Pressure and the solenoidal projection}\label{ssec:ns-projection}
Contracting \eqref{eq:ns-nondim-momentum-fourier} with $k_i$ and using the
continuity constraint gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-pressure-contracted}
  0 = -\sigma_L \left( k_i k_j \frac{\partial \hat{u}_i}{\partial k_j} + d\, k_i \hat{u}_i \right)
  - \sigma_U\, k_i \hat{u}_i
  - k_i \hat{N}_i
  - \mathrm{i} k^2 \hat{p}
  + k_i \hat{G}_i
  - k_i \hat{F}_i.
\end{equation}
The remaining scaling contribution also vanishes: differentiating
\eqref{eq:ns-fourier-continuity} with respect to $k_j$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-continuity-dk}
  \frac{\partial}{\partial k_j}\left( k_i \hat{u}_i \right)
  = \hat{u}_j + k_i \frac{\partial \hat{u}_i}{\partial k_j} = 0
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  k_i \frac{\partial \hat{u}_i}{\partial k_j} = -\hat{u}_j,
\end{equation}
so that $k_i k_j \partial \hat{u}_i / \partial k_j = -k_j \hat{u}_j = 0$.
Hence the scaling terms are divergence free on their own --- the self-similar
frame does not generate pressure --- and \eqref{eq:ns-pressure-contracted}
reduces to a Poisson equation for the pressure. Unlike a constant-coefficient
viscous term, the subgrid stress is not divergence free, $k_i \hat{G}_i =
\mathrm{i} k_i k_j \hat{T}_{ij} \neq 0$ in general, and neither is the sponge
forcing, $k_i \hat{F}_i = -\mathrm{i}\, \widehat{u_i \partial_i W}$
(\S\ref{ssec:ns-sponge}), so both enter the source alongside the convection,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-fourier-poisson}
  \mathrm{i} k^2 \hat{p} = -k_i \left( \hat{N}_i - \hat{G}_i + \hat{F}_i \right)
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \hat{p} = \mathrm{i}\,\frac{k_l \left( \hat{N}_l - \hat{G}_l + \hat{F}_l \right)}{k^2},
  \qquad k \neq 0,
\end{equation}
with $\hat{p}$ at $k = 0$ fixed arbitrarily, since only its gradient enters
the momentum equation. Substituting \eqref{eq:ns-fourier-poisson} into
\eqref{eq:ns-nondim-momentum-fourier}, the pressure term becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-fourier-pressure-substituted}
  -\mathrm{i} k_i \hat{p} = \frac{k_i k_l}{k^2} \left( \hat{N}_l - \hat{G}_l + \hat{F}_l \right),
\end{equation}
which combines with $-\hat{N}_i + \hat{G}_i - \hat{F}_i$ to give the solenoidal
projection of the convective, subgrid stress, and sponge terms together. Defining the projection tensor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-projection-tensor}
  P_{il}(\mathbf{k}) = \delta_{il} - \frac{k_i k_l}{k^2},
\end{equation}
the evolution equation splits exactly along the lines of the lemma
\eqref{eq:fw-lemma},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-fourier-final}
  \frac{\partial \hat{u}_i}{\partial t}
  = \underbrace{-\sigma_L \left( k_j \frac{\partial \hat{u}_i}{\partial k_j} + 3\, \hat{u}_i \right)
  - \sigma_U\, \hat{u}_i}_{\text{scaling terms}}
  \underbrace{- P_{il}(\mathbf{k}) \left( \hat{N}_l - \hat{G}_l + \hat{F}_l \right)}_{\text{physical terms}}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{The physics step}\label{ssec:ns-physics-step}

The physics operator \eqref{eq:fw-physics-flow} is the flow of the bracketed
physical terms of \eqref{eq:ns-fourier-final},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-physics-flow}
  \Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{P})}\bigl(\hat{u}_i(t)\bigr)
  = \hat{u}_i(t) - \int_{t}^{t+\Delta t}
    P_{il}(\mathbf{k}) \left( \hat{N}_l - \hat{G}_l + \hat{F}_l \right) \mathrm{d}t .
\end{equation}
The sponge is thus part of the physics operator, not a separate substep: it is
switched off by setting $W_{\max} = 0$, independently of \textsc{ApplyBC}
(\S\ref{ssec:ns-boundary}).

Unlike the 1D case, \eqref{eq:ns-physics-flow} has no closed form: the
convective and subgrid stress terms are both nonlinear, so $\Phi^{(\mathrm{P})}$ must be realized by an
actual time-marching scheme. Two things constrain the choice. It must meet the
accuracy requirement of \S\ref{ssec:alg-substeps} --- second order for the
Strang composition to retain its order --- and it must handle the dealiasing of
the pseudo-spectral products \eqref{eq:ns-fourier-convection} and
\eqref{eq:ns-fourier-sgs}.

We take $\Phi^{(\mathrm{P})}$ to be a single step of the classical fourth-order
Runge--Kutta method (RK4) applied to the full physical right-hand side, the
subgrid stress term $\hat{G}_i$ and the sponge forcing $\hat{F}_i$ marched
together with the convection. It is set out in Appendix~\ref{app:rk4},
together with the stability limits it places on $\Delta t$. The advective limit
depends on the state, and is enforced by the CFL time step of
\S\ref{ssec:alg-timestep}, through \textsc{MaxStableDt}
\eqref{eq:rk4-cfl-dt}. The sponge limit \eqref{eq:rk4-sponge-limit} is fixed
by $W_{\max}$ and is assumed rather than enforced.

\subsection{Constraint functionals}\label{ssec:ns-constraints}

The moments \eqref{eq:1d-functionals} used for the 1D equation do not carry
over: a velocity field in a periodic box has
$\int u_i\, \mathrm{d}\mathbf{x} \approx 0$, so $F_1$ as written is not usable
as a scale. We therefore take the corresponding moments of the energy density
$u_i u_i$, which is non-negative and so cannot cancel,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-functionals}
  F_1[u] = \int u_i u_i\, \mathrm{d}\mathbf{x} \equiv E_0,
  \qquad
  F_2[u] = \int r^2\, u_i u_i\, \mathrm{d}\mathbf{x} \equiv E_2,
\end{equation}
where $r^2 = x_j x_j$ is the squared distance from the centre of the domain
\eqref{eq:alg-grid}. Thus $E_0$ is twice the total kinetic energy and
$E_2 / E_0$ the energy-weighted mean square radius, so $E_0$ measures the
amplitude of the field and $E_2$ its spread, exactly as $M_0$ and $M_2$ do in
the 1D case.
\par
Writing $E_0^*, E_2^*$ for the values carried by the field being scaled and
substituting $\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{x}\, \tilde L$ in each integral, with
$d = 3$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-E0-homogeneity}
  \int \frac{1}{\tilde U^2}\, u_i u_i\!\left( \mathbf{x}\, \tilde L \right) \mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}
  = \frac{1}{\tilde U^2 \tilde L^{3}}\, E_0^*,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-E2-homogeneity}
  \int r^2\, \frac{1}{\tilde U^2}\, u_i u_i\!\left( \mathbf{x}\, \tilde L \right) \mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}
  = \frac{1}{\tilde U^2 \tilde L^{5}}\, E_2^*,
\end{equation}
so the homogeneity exponents of \eqref{eq:fw-homogeneity} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-exponents}
  (p_1, q_1) = (2, 3), \qquad (p_2, q_2) = (2, 5),
\end{equation}
with $p_1 q_2 - p_2 q_1 = 4 \neq 0$, so the constraint system
\eqref{eq:fw-constraint-system} is uniquely solvable. Solving it,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-Ltilde}
  \tilde L = \sqrt{\frac{E_0\, E_2^*}{E_0^*\, E_2}},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-Utilde}
  \tilde U = \left(\frac{E_0^*}{E_0}\right)^{1/2} \tilde L^{-3/2}
  = \left(\frac{E_0^*}{E_0}\right)^{5/4} \left(\frac{E_2}{E_2^*}\right)^{3/4} .
\end{equation}
The length ratio \eqref{eq:ns-Ltilde} is identical in form to its 1D
counterpart \eqref{eq:1d-Ltilde}: in both cases $\tilde L$ is the square root
of a ratio of mean square widths.

\emph{Open.} Two properties of this pair are assumptions rather than
consequences. First, the homogeneity \eqref{eq:fw-homogeneity} is exact only
for integrals over $\mathbb{R}^d$; on the finite box the substitution
$\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{x}\, \tilde L$ rescales the domain of integration as
well, so \eqref{eq:ns-E0-homogeneity} and \eqref{eq:ns-E2-homogeneity} hold
only to the extent that the energy is concentrated well inside the box. This
is the same assumption the 1D moments make, and like it is not currently
monitored. Second, $r$ is measured from a fixed origin and is not periodic, so
$E_2$ is meaningful only for a field that stays centred and localized: for a
statistically homogeneous field filling the box, $u_i u_i$ is uniform on
average and $E_2 / E_0$ reduces to the mean square radius of the box itself,
carrying no information about the field. Which of these two regimes the
intended runs occupy should be settled before the pair is used.

\subsection{Initial condition}\label{ssec:ns-ic}
Because the curl of a vector feild is divergence-free we will define $u_i$ as the curl of some feild $A_i$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-ic-curl}
  u_i = \varepsilon_{ijk} \frac{\partial A_k}{\partial x_j},
  \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
  \hat{u}_i = \mathrm{i}\, \varepsilon_{ijk}\, k_j \hat{A}_k.
\end{equation}
$u_i$ is built on the grid \eqref{eq:alg-grid} in four steps, followed
by a rescaling.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Noise.} Each component $A_k(\mathbf{x_n})$ is an independent
  standard normal, drawn from a seeded Mersenne Twister.  
  \item \emph{Spectral filter.} The noise is given a spectrum,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-ic-filter}
    \hat{A}_k \gets \hat{A}_k\, e^{-k^2/k_p^2}.
  \end{equation}
  \item \emph{Window.} The potential is localized in physical space,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-ic-window}
    A_k \gets A_k\, e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2},
  \end{equation}
  with $r$ the same distance from the centre of the domain that $E_2$ uses in
  \eqref{eq:ns-functionals}.  
  \item \emph{Rescaling.} Finally $u_i \gets u_i \sqrt{E_0 / F_1[u]}$, with
  $F_1$ evaluated as the Riemann sum \eqref{eq:alg-riemann}, so that the field
  delivered to the integrator carries a prescribed $E_0$. The constraint
  targets \eqref{eq:alg-targets} are then $F_1 = E_0$ by construction and
  $F_2 = E_2[u_0]$, the latter set by $\sigma$ rather than chosen
  independently.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Spectral interpolation in three dimensions}\label{ssec:ns-interpolation}

The scaling operator \eqref{eq:fw-scale-step} needs the same band-limited
evaluation \eqref{eq:fw-spectral-interp} as in one dimension, but at $d = 3$ a
direct evaluation of it is no longer affordable, and its realization on the
real transform differs enough from \eqref{eq:1d-interp} to be worth writing
out.

\paragraph{The sum on the real transform.}
Write $\hat u_i^{(k)}(\mathbf{j}) = N^{-3}\,\mathrm{RFFT}(u_k)_{\mathbf{j}}$
for the normalized coefficients, with the first axis carrying only the
non-negative frequencies $j_1 = 0, 1, \dots, N/2$ and the remaining two
carrying the full signed set $j_d = 0, 1, \dots, N/2, -(N/2-1), \dots, -1$.
Two corrections turn \eqref{eq:fw-spectral-interp} into a sum over that
layout. First, the omitted Hermitian conjugates are restored by the
multiplicity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-interp-multiplicity}
  m_{j_1} =
  \begin{cases}
    1, & j_1 = 0, \ \text{or } j_1 = N/2 \text{ with } N \text{ even}, \\
    2, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
exactly as the factor of $2$ does in \eqref{eq:1d-interp}. Second, the
transform phases its coefficients to the sample index $n$, which on the
centred grid \eqref{eq:alg-grid} places its origin at $x = -H/2$ rather than
at $x = 0$. Since the scaling operator dilates about the centre of the domain,
each mode must be evaluated at $\tilde L x + H/2$, not at
$\tilde L (x + H/2)$ --- the two agree only at $\tilde L = 1$. As $H/2$ is
half a period, the correction is the constant phase
$\exp(\mathrm{i} k_j H/2) = (-1)^{j}$ per axis. Together,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-interp}
  \Phi_{\Delta t}^{(\mathrm{S})}(u_k)_i(\mathbf{x})
  = \frac{1}{\tilde U_{k+1}} \operatorname{Re}
    \sum_{j_1 = 0}^{N/2} \sum_{j_2} \sum_{j_3}
      m_{j_1}\, \hat u_i^{(k)}(\mathbf{j})
      \prod_{d=1}^{3} (-1)^{j_d}
      \exp\!\left( \frac{2\pi \mathrm{i}\, j_d\, \tilde L_{k+1}\, x_d}{H} \right).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Separable evaluation.}
Evaluated as written, \eqref{eq:ns-interp} costs $O(N^{2d})$: every retained
mode is summed at every grid point, which at $N = 128$ is $4 \times 10^{12}$
complex multiply--adds per scaling substep. The kernel, however, is a product
of one factor per axis, so the triple sum separates and can be applied one
axis at a time. Define the two interpolation matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-interp-matrices}
  W^{(1)}_{n j_1} = m_{j_1} (-1)^{j_1}
    \exp\!\left( \frac{2\pi \mathrm{i}\, j_1 \tilde L_{k+1} x_n}{H} \right),
  \qquad
  W^{(\mathrm{f})}_{n j} = (-1)^{j}
    \exp\!\left( \frac{2\pi \mathrm{i}\, j\, \tilde L_{k+1} x_n}{H} \right),
\end{equation}
the first of size $N \times (N/2 + 1)$ for the half-spectrum axis and the
second of size $N \times N$ for the two full axes, both rebuilt whenever
$\tilde L_{k+1}$ changes. Then \eqref{eq:ns-interp} is the sequence of
contractions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-interp-contractions}
  \hat u_i \;\xrightarrow{\ W^{(\mathrm{f})} \text{ on axis } 3\ }\;
  \;\xrightarrow{\ W^{(\mathrm{f})} \text{ on axis } 2\ }\;
  \;\xrightarrow{\ W^{(1)} \text{ on axis } 1\ }\;
  \operatorname{Re}(\cdot),
\end{equation}
each of which is a matrix--matrix product once the contracted axis is brought
to the front. The cost falls to $O(d\, N^{d+1})$, i.e.\ $8 \times 10^{8}$
multiply--adds at $N = 128$, a factor of $5 \times 10^{3}$ below the direct
sum, and the work is expressed as three dense $\mathrm{GEMM}$ calls. The
factorization is exact --- it is a regrouping of the same finite sum, not an
approximation --- so the guarantee of \S\ref{ssec:fw-interpolation} that the
scaling step contributes no spatial discretization error is retained. For
$d = 1$ the sequence \eqref{eq:ns-interp-contractions} collapses to the single
contraction with $W^{(1)}$, which is \eqref{eq:1d-interp}.

\paragraph{The Nyquist bin.}
For even $N$ the bins $j_d = \pm N/2$ of a real field's transform coincide,
and the two axis layouts resolve that differently. On the half-spectrum axis
the mode is stored once and is given multiplicity $m_{N/2} = 1$ by
\eqref{eq:ns-interp-multiplicity}. On a full axis it is stored in the single
bin $j_d = N/2$ but represents the conjugate pair, so it must be split evenly
between $+N/2$ and $-N/2$; the two halves combine to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-interp-nyquist}
  W^{(\mathrm{f})}_{n,\, N/2} = (-1)^{N/2}
    \cos\!\left( \frac{\pi N \tilde L_{k+1} x_n}{H} \right),
\end{equation}
the same cosine that closes \eqref{eq:1d-interp}. With
\eqref{eq:ns-interp-nyquist} in place the contraction
\eqref{eq:ns-interp-contractions} reproduces the direct sum
\eqref{eq:ns-interp} to roundoff for either parity of $N$.

\paragraph{The out-of-box cutoff.}
The box indicator $\chi$ of \eqref{eq:fw-dilate-cutoff} is a product of one
factor per axis, like the kernel, so it separates the same way. It is imposed by
zeroing row $n$ of each interpolation matrix \eqref{eq:ns-interp-matrices}
whenever the query of that axis leaves the box,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-interp-cutoff}
  W^{(1)}_{n j_1} \gets 0, \quad W^{(\mathrm{f})}_{n j} \gets 0
  \qquad \text{whenever } |x_n \tilde L_{k+1}| > H/2 .
\end{equation}
After the three contractions \eqref{eq:ns-interp-contractions} the point
$\mathbf{x_n}$ then carries the product of the three row factors, which is
$\chi(\mathbf{x_n}\tilde L_{k+1})$ exactly. The cutoff is thus applied at no
cost beyond building the matrices, and the direct sum it is tested against
applies $\chi$ pointwise. For $\tilde L_{k+1}$ within a few percent of unity
the zeroed rows are the outermost one or two grid points per side. These lie
deep in the sponge layer (\S\ref{ssec:ns-sponge}), where the field has already
been damped, and \textsc{ApplyBC}, when enabled, clamps most of them anyway
(\S\ref{ssec:ns-boundary}). Zeroing a region in physical space is not a
solenoidal operation, so, like the clamp, it reintroduces a divergence
proportional to the amplitude it removes.

\paragraph{Interaction with dealiasing.}
The dilation maps each represented wavenumber to $\tilde L_{k+1} \mathbf{k}$,
so a field that fills the spectrum up to the Nyquist wavenumber would wrap
under any $\tilde L_{k+1} > 1$. The $2/3$ rule already required by the
pseudo-spectral product \eqref{eq:ns-fourier-convection} supplies the margin:
the top retained mode sits at $N/3$ and moves to $\tilde L_{k+1} N/3$, which
stays below $N/2$ for every $\tilde L_{k+1} < 3/2$. Runs in the self-similar
frame hold $\tilde L_{k+1}$ within a few percent of unity, so the margin is
not approached, but it is the condition under which the interpolation stays
free of aliasing. Finally, \eqref{eq:ns-interp-contractions} is applied to
each velocity component independently and mixes neither components nor the
wavenumbers within an axis; at the exceptional value $\tilde L_{k+1} = 1$ it
reduces to the plain inverse transform, which is taken instead at
$O(N^d \log N)$.

\subsection{Boundary treatment}\label{ssec:ns-boundary}

Two boundary treatments are available, and each can be switched on or off
independently of the other. The first is the sponge forcing of
\S\ref{ssec:ns-sponge}, which is part of the physics step and is switched off
by $W_{\max} = 0$. The second is the clamp described below, applied by
\textsc{ApplyBC} after each step and switched off by a run flag, in which case
\textsc{ApplyBC} is the identity. The clamp was the original treatment; the
sponge is intended to replace it. The intended configuration is therefore
sponge on, clamp off, and the clamp is retained so that the two can be
compared: if the sponge alone keeps $u_i$ negligible at the faces, the clamp,
and the divergence it introduces, are no longer needed.

\paragraph{The clamp.}
When enabled, \textsc{ApplyBC} imposes a homogeneous
Dirichlet condition on the velocity by clamping the outer layer of grid points
of the centred domain \eqref{eq:alg-grid} after each completed step,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-clamp}
  u_i(\mathbf{x_n}) \gets 0 \qquad \text{whenever } n_d \in \{0,\, N-1\}
  \text{ for some direction } d,
\end{equation}
for every component $i = 1, 2, 3$. As in one dimension, on the centred grid
$x_d \in [-H/2, H/2)$ the layers $n_d = 0$ and $n_d = N-1$ are distinct grid
points rather than periodic images of one another, so both are clamped; the
six layers together are the boundary of the box.

\emph{Open.} The clamp carries the tension of \S\ref{ssec:1d-boundary} over
to the Navier--Stokes case, and sharpens it. As in one dimension, the discretization
presumes periodicity --- the pseudo-spectral evaluation of
\eqref{eq:ns-fourier-final} does, and the spectral interpolation
\eqref{eq:ns-interp} does inside the box, though its cutoff
\eqref{eq:ns-interp-cutoff} returns zero outside it --- while
\eqref{eq:ns-clamp} imposes a Dirichlet condition, and the two are consistent only so long as $u_i$ is
negligible near the faces. Beyond that, the clamp is not a solenoidal
perturbation: \eqref{eq:ns-fourier-continuity} is enforced in Fourier space,
and zeroing a layer of grid points in physical space reintroduces a
divergence, so the field delivered to the next step no longer satisfies the
continuity equation exactly. The size of that violation is set by the
amplitude the clamp removes, and therefore by the same localization
assumption the constraint pair of \S\ref{ssec:ns-constraints} already makes:
for a puff held several widths inside the box it is at roundoff, and it grows
with the fraction of $E_0$ the boundary layer carries. With the sponge on,
that fraction is itself suppressed by the damping in the layer, so the clamp
has little left to remove.

\paragraph{Optional Leray projection.}
The violation can instead be removed outright. With the Leray option enabled,
\textsc{ApplyBC} follows the clamp \eqref{eq:ns-clamp} by projecting the
clamped field back onto the solenoidal subspace with the projection tensor
\eqref{eq:ns-projection-tensor},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ns-leray-bc}
  \hat{u}_i(\mathbf{k}) \gets P_{il}(\mathbf{k})\, \hat{u}_l(\mathbf{k})
  = \hat{u}_i - \frac{k_i k_l \hat{u}_l}{k^2},
  \qquad \mathbf{k} \neq \mathbf{0},
\end{equation}
and transforming back to physical space. The mean mode $\mathbf{k} = \mathbf{0}$
is left unchanged, and the Nyquist bins --- which have no representable
derivative, and on which $P_{il}$ does not respect the conjugate symmetry of
the real transform --- are set to zero. The 2/3 dealiasing mask is not applied
here: the next physics step dealiases its input anyway
(\S\ref{ssec:ns-physics-step}), and applying the mask here would remove far more
than the clamp changed. After \eqref{eq:ns-leray-bc} the discrete divergence
$k_i \hat{u}_i$ is at roundoff, and because $P_{il}$ is an orthogonal
projection it can only lower $E_0$.

What this gives up is the clamp itself. $P_{il}$ is nonlocal in physical space:
the gradient part it removes is the gradient of a potential $\phi$ solving
$\nabla^2 \phi = \nabla \cdot \mathbf{u}$, and that potential is spread
over the whole box. So the boundary layers of \eqref{eq:ns-clamp} come back
out of \eqref{eq:ns-leray-bc} with small nonzero values, and a slowly decaying
potential-flow tail is added to the field --- the same effect
\S\ref{ssec:ns-ic} notes for projecting a windowed initial condition. The two
treatments are therefore complementary: the clamp alone keeps the faces exactly
at zero and allows a divergence proportional to the amplitude it removes, while
the clamp followed by \eqref{eq:ns-leray-bc} keeps the field exactly solenoidal
and lets the faces move away from zero by the same order. Both are small only
when $u_i$ is negligible near the faces, so the localization monitored by
\texttt{leakage} is still needed either way. The option is off by default.

\section{The RK4 physics step}\label{app:rk4}

This appendix sets out the scheme used for the Navier--Stokes physics operator
$\Phi^{(\mathrm{P})}$ of \S\ref{ssec:ns-physics-step}: one step of the classical
fourth-order Runge--Kutta method. Throughout we abbreviate the step as
$h \equiv \Delta t$, reserving $\Delta$ for the filter width of the subgrid
stress model of \S\ref{ssec:ns-viscosity}.

\subsection{The scheme}\label{ssec:rk4-scheme}

Written out mode by mode, the physics flow \eqref{eq:ns-physics-flow} is the
system of ordinary differential equations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rk4-rhs}
  \frac{\mathrm{d}\hat{u}_i}{\mathrm{d}t} = R_i(\hat{u}),
  \qquad
  R_i(\hat{u}) = -P_{il}(\mathbf{k}) \left( \hat{N}_l - \hat{G}_l + \hat{F}_l \right) ,
\end{equation}
the bracketed physical terms of \eqref{eq:ns-fourier-final}. RK4 advances it
through four evaluations of $R$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rk4-stages}
  \begin{aligned}
    \hat{k}^{(1)}_i &= R_i\bigl(\hat{u}^{(n)}\bigr), &
    \hat{k}^{(2)}_i &= R_i\bigl(\hat{u}^{(n)} + \tfrac{h}{2}\hat{k}^{(1)}\bigr), \\
    \hat{k}^{(3)}_i &= R_i\bigl(\hat{u}^{(n)} + \tfrac{h}{2}\hat{k}^{(2)}\bigr), &
    \hat{k}^{(4)}_i &= R_i\bigl(\hat{u}^{(n)} + h\,\hat{k}^{(3)}\bigr),
  \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
combined with Simpson weights,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rk4-update}
  \hat{u}_i^{(n+1)} = \hat{u}_i^{(n)}
    + \frac{h}{6}\left( \hat{k}^{(1)}_i + 2\hat{k}^{(2)}_i
      + 2\hat{k}^{(3)}_i + \hat{k}^{(4)}_i \right).
\end{equation}
The convective part of each evaluation is computed pseudo-spectrally in
rotational form, $u_j \partial_j u_i = -(\mathbf{u} \times \boldsymbol{\omega})_i
+ \partial_i (u_k u_k/2)$, whose gradient part $P_{il}$ annihilates; this costs
six inverse and three forward transforms. The subgrid stress term
\eqref{eq:ns-fourier-sgs} costs six further inverse transforms, one per
independent component of $S_{ij}$, and six forward transforms of $T_{ij}$.
The sponge term \eqref{eq:ns-fourier-sponge} reuses the physical-space velocity
already formed for the convective term and multiplies it by the tabulated $W$,
so it costs only the three forward transforms of $W u_i$. An evaluation thus
costs $24$ transforms and a step $96$ in all. The scheme has no coefficients beyond the four weights, so nothing is
precomputed or cached.

\paragraph{Order.} The scheme is fourth order in $h$, so it satisfies the
second-order requirement \S\ref{ssec:alg-substeps} places on \textsc{Evolve}
for the Strang composition with a margin.

\subsection{Stability}\label{ssec:rk4-stability}

Applied to the linear model $\mathrm{d}\hat{u}/\mathrm{d}t = \lambda \hat{u}$,
\eqref{eq:rk4-stages}--\eqref{eq:rk4-update} multiply $\hat{u}$ by the
amplification factor
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rk4-polynomial}
  A(z) = 1 + z + \frac{z^2}{2} + \frac{z^3}{6} + \frac{z^4}{24},
  \qquad z = \lambda h,
\end{equation}
the degree-four Taylor polynomial of $e^{z}$, and the step is stable while
$|A(z)| \le 1$.

\paragraph{The advective limit.} The convective term is, locally, a
transport at speed $|\mathbf{u}|$, whose eigenvalues on the retained band lie
on the imaginary axis with $|\lambda| \le \max|\mathbf{u}|\, k_{\max}$, where
$k_{\max} = 2\pi (N/3)/H$ is the top mode retained by the $2/3$ rule. Along
the imaginary axis $|A(z)| \le 1$ holds for $|z| \le 2\sqrt{2}$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rk4-advective-limit}
  h\, \max|\mathbf{u}|\, k_{\max} \le 2\sqrt{2} .
\end{equation}
The self-similar frame pins $\max|\mathbf{u}| = O(1)$ on average, so the
limit does not drift over a run as it would in a decaying simulation in
physical variables. It does not pin it pointwise in time, however: the field
can momentarily concentrate into a high-amplitude structure whose
$\max|\mathbf{u}|$ carries a step that is stable in the settled regime past
\eqref{eq:rk4-advective-limit}.

\paragraph{The sponge limit.} The sponge forcing \eqref{eq:ns-sponge-force}
is, pointwise, a linear decay at rate $W(r) \in [0, W_{\max}]$, whose
eigenvalues lie on the negative real axis with $|\lambda| \le W_{\max}$. Along
the negative real axis $|A(z)| \le 1$ holds for $-2.785 \lesssim z \le 0$, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rk4-sponge-limit}
  h\, W_{\max} \le 2.785 .
\end{equation}
Unlike \eqref{eq:rk4-advective-limit}, this limit does not depend on the state:
it is fixed by the run parameter $W_{\max}$. It is not enforced by
\textsc{MaxStableDt}, but assumed. At the default $W_{\max} = 10$ and a
CFL-limited step of $h \approx 0.05$, $h\, W_{\max} \approx 0.5$, well inside
both \eqref{eq:rk4-sponge-limit} and the range $h\, W_{\max} \lesssim 1$ in
which the step also follows the exponential decay $e^{-W h}$ accurately. A run
with a larger $W_{\max}$ or a longer step must check
\eqref{eq:rk4-sponge-limit} by hand.

\paragraph{The CFL time step.} The limit is therefore enforced, by the
step rule \eqref{eq:alg-dt-rule} of \S\ref{ssec:alg-timestep}. Its CFL term,
\textsc{MaxStableDt}, is the step that puts
\eqref{eq:rk4-advective-limit} at the fraction $C$ of its limit,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rk4-cfl-dt}
  \Delta t_{\mathrm{CFL}}(u; C) = C\, \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\max|\mathbf{u}|\, k_{\max}},
\end{equation}
and the realized CFL \eqref{eq:alg-realized-cfl} is
$\Delta t_k \max|\mathbf{u}_{k+1}|\, k_{\max} / 2\sqrt{2}$.

\paragraph{A resolution margin.} A structure that concentrates below the
grid scale also raises $\max|\mathbf{u}|$, and so looks like a CFL problem;
but a smaller step only delays it. To tell the two apart the run also records
the fraction of the dealiased energy $\tfrac12 \sum |\hat{u}|^2$ carried by
$|\mathbf{k}| > \tfrac23 k_{\max}$. It stays small while the field is
resolved and rises as energy piles up against the $2/3$ cutoff. A realized CFL
excursion with a small tail fraction is a time-step problem; one preceded by a
rising tail fraction is a resolution problem.

\subsection{Solenoidality}\label{ssec:rk4-divergence}

The step contributes nothing of its own to the continuity residual. Every
contribution to $R_i$ --- convective, subgrid, and sponge alike --- has had
$P_{il}$ applied,
and $k_i P_{il} = k_l - k_l k^2/k^2 = 0$, so contracting \eqref{eq:rk4-rhs}
with $k_i$ gives $k_i R_i(\hat{u}) = 0$. Each stage of \eqref{eq:rk4-stages}
is then divergence free, and the divergence of each mode is carried through the
step unchanged,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rk4-divergence}
  k_i \hat{u}_i^{(n+1)} = k_i \hat{u}_i^{(n)} .
\end{equation}
Whatever divergence enters the field is therefore neither amplified nor
damped by the step, and if \eqref{eq:ns-fourier-continuity} holds on input it
holds on output. The residual
the run log records is therefore entirely the boundary clamp's, when the clamp
is enabled (\S\ref{ssec:ns-boundary}), and not the time stepper's.

\subsection{Dealiasing}\label{ssec:rk4-dealiasing}

The $2/3$ mask of \S\ref{ssec:ns-fourier} is applied to the incoming field
before the first stage. No stage then leaves the retained band: the convective,
subgrid stress, and sponge terms are masked after their forward transforms, the
projection $P_{il}$ is diagonal in $\mathbf{k}$, and each stage argument in
\eqref{eq:rk4-stages} is a linear combination of band-limited fields. The mask
is applied once more to $\hat{u}^{(n+1)}$, which changes nothing in exact
arithmetic but makes explicit that no content reaches the band the next scaling
step will stretch.
